# CITY MASTER TASTE — ODAIBA 2127

**Status:** Master visual / architectural direction  
**Use:** Mandatory reference for every building evolution, new object, mobility system, landscape intervention, public space, prop, and future city asset in Odaiba 2127.

---

## 0. Purpose

This document defines the **visual taste and world logic of Odaiba 2127**.

It exists to prevent the project from drifting into:

- renovated 2027 architecture,
- generic premium smart-city design,
- generic sci-fi,
- cyberpunk,
- contemporary glass-and-steel architecture with futuristic props added afterward,
- or unrelated futuristic objects that do not belong to the same city.

Every new object and every evolution of an existing building must feel as if it belongs to **one continuous 100-year urban lineage**.

The key question is not:

> “Does this look futuristic?”

The key question is:

> **“Could this only belong to Odaiba 2127?”**

# 2. World premise

Odaiba 2127 is not a preserved 2027 district with futuristic additions.

Over one century, the architectural ideas of the original Fuji TV building have evolved into the **operating system of an entire waterfront district**.

The original Fuji TV building is therefore treated as **genetic origin**, not as an object that must be copied.

Its deeper architectural DNA is:

- exposed structural frame,
- object suspended inside frame,
- circle / sphere,
- square grid,
- diagonal circulation,
- large voids,
- elevated connections,
- visible infrastructure,
- civic monumentality.

By 2127 these ideas have expanded into:

- district-scale urban chassis,
- suspended civic ecosystems,
- hybrid public infrastructure,
- three-dimensional mobility networks,
- ring and circular interchange systems,
- adaptive waterfront architecture,
- ecological infrastructure,
- public spaces distributed across multiple vertical levels.

The desired result should feel like:

> **Fuji TV → architectural DNA → district-wide urban evolution**

not:

> Fuji TV → futuristic renovation.

---

# 3. Core design principle

## RADICAL SYSTEMS, RESTRAINED AESTHETICS

The future should come from **changed urban systems**, not decorative sci-fi styling.

The city can be radically different in:

- structure,
- movement,
- ecology,
- waterfront logic,
- public-space organization,
- building typology,
- media systems,
- logistics,
- energy and climate response.

But the aesthetic remains controlled.

Avoid:

- excessive neon,
- cyberpunk color,
- arbitrary glowing objects,
- random flying vehicles,
- sculptural sci-fi blobs,
- visual clutter,
- fantasy anti-gravity forms.

The future is radical because the **city operates differently**, not because every surface is visually loud.

---

# 4. The 2127 test

Every major design must pass this question:

> **If the futuristic vehicles and lighting were removed, would the architecture and urban system still be impossible to mistake for 2027?**

If the answer is **no**, the design is not futuristic enough.

A successful design should remain recognizably 2127 in clear daylight.

---

# 5. Replace typologies — do not decorate them

Do not futurize the appearance of 2027 urban typologies.

Replace the typologies themselves.

### Avoid
- conventional road
- ordinary car street
- bus stop
- monorail station
- office tower
- shopping mall
- glass business campus
- slab + cylindrical column system
- planter tree
- rooftop garden
- conventional ferry pier
- escalator bank
- skybridge between two towers

### Prefer
- post-road civic terrain
- three-dimensional mobility field
- magnetic structural transit
- multimodal civic interchange
- inhabited megaframe
- hybrid civic volume
- suspended public ecosystem
- branching / transfer structural systems
- climate ecology
- ecological infrastructure
- amphibious mobility node
- diagonal inhabited circulation
- continuous elevated civic network

The difference must be structural and spatial, not cosmetic.

---

# 6. Architectural DNA

Every building does **not** need to look the same.

Every building does need to feel genetically related.

Use the DNA through **rules**, not repetition.

## 6.1 Frame

The frame may become:

- urban chassis,
- deep structural truss,
- inhabited structural member,
- mobility-support framework,
- transfer structure,
- large-scale public skeleton,
- suspended infrastructure carrier.

Avoid repeating simple white rectangular frames without functional purpose.

## 6.2 Circle / sphere

Do not place identical silver spheres everywhere.

The circular lineage may evolve into:

- partial spheres,
- hollow rings,
- circular voids,
- sliced spherical chambers,
- ring transit nodes,
- botanical climate chambers,
- civic media chambers,
- structural transfer rings,
- circular water basins,
- observation environments.

Use circular geometry as a **family of typologies**, not a logo.

## 6.3 Diagonal movement

Diagonal movement is one of the strongest pieces of Fuji-derived DNA.

It may appear as:

- inhabited slopes,
- diagonal magnetic mobility,
- ramps large enough to become public space,
- inclined gardens,
- diagonal civic streets,
- suspended routes,
- transfer systems crossing multiple levels.

Avoid using ordinary escalators as the main expression.

## 6.4 Void

Large voids are essential.

The district should not become visually solid.

Use voids to:

- frame sky,
- expose Tokyo Bay,
- allow aerial routes,
- create civic scale,
- separate suspended volumes,
- reveal multiple city layers,
- maintain openness.

---

# 7. Density rule

## MORE LAYERED, NOT MORE CROWDED

Odaiba must retain:

- open sky,
- long sightlines,
- visible water,
- large negative spaces,
- generous separation between major structures,
- major structural voids.

Future intensity should come from:

- vertical layering,
- suspended spaces,
- mobility systems,
- ecological layers,
- elevated decks,
- amphibious waterfront systems.

Do not solve futurism by filling the skyline with towers.

---

# 8. Building evolution rules

When evolving an existing Odaiba building:

## Keep
- useful spatial memory,
- landmark relationship,
- site role,
- important urban alignment,
- meaningful architectural DNA.

## Transform
- building typology,
- circulation,
- structural logic,
- public interface,
- mobility integration,
- ecological role,
- envelope behavior,
- vertical relationship with the district.

Do not simply:

- enlarge the old building,
- add a glass shell,
- add a rooftop garden,
- attach flying-car docks,
- add LED screens,
- wrap it with futuristic bridges.

A 2127 evolution should answer:

1. What new role does this structure serve?
2. What urban systems now pass through it?
3. What can it do that a 2027 building cannot?
4. How does it participate in the larger civic chassis?
5. How does it remain related to Odaiba / Fuji architectural lineage?

---

# 9. New building rules

A new building must not look like a random futuristic insertion.

Before designing it, define:

### Function
What civic / ecological / mobility / cultural role does it perform?

### Hybridization
Which present-day building categories have merged?

Examples:

- station + garden + public forum,
- media chamber + observation environment,
- mobility node + climate infrastructure,
- research space + public ecology,
- housing + public deck + mobility support.

### Structural relationship
How does it connect to:

- megaframe,
- elevated deck,
- mobility network,
- waterfront,
- ecological infrastructure?

### Fuji lineage
Which deeper architectural rule does it inherit?

Not necessarily a sphere.

It may inherit:

- suspended object,
- grid logic,
- diagonal movement,
- large void,
- circular geometry,
- exposed structure.

---

# 10. New object / prop rules

Small objects must also belong to the world.

Do not create a futuristic version of a 2027 prop unless there is a reason.

For every new object, ask:

> “What changed in the system around this object over 100 years?”

Examples:

### Bench
Do not design “a futuristic bench.”

Instead, seating may be integrated into:

- civic terrain,
- structural edge,
- ecological climate zone,
- mobility waiting environment.

### Lamp
Avoid decorative sci-fi lamp posts.

Lighting should often be integrated into:

- structural members,
- mobility paths,
- climate canopy,
- public surfaces.

### Kiosk
Avoid a 2027 kiosk with a futuristic skin.

Use:

- civic interface chamber,
- embedded information surface,
- mobility / service node.

### Tree planter
Avoid isolated decorative planter boxes.

Use:

- deep ecological soil systems,
- climate corridor,
- habitat infrastructure,
- water-retention landscape.

---

# 11. Structural taste

Avoid repetitive:

- cylindrical columns,
- flat slab-and-column construction,
- conventional glass office cores,
- structurally unexplained floating masses.

Prefer:

- deep-space trusses,
- giant transfer structures,
- branching composite supports,
- tension / compression systems,
- structural rings,
- long-span systems,
- inhabited structural members,
- suspended volumes,
- visible joints,
- believable load paths.

Radical architecture must still look engineered.

The viewer should feel:

> “I do not know how we would build this today, but I understand how it stands.”

---

# 12. Mobility taste

Transportation is one of the strongest visual markers of 2127.

Use a layered mobility ecology.

## 12.1 Structural magnetic mobility

Primary district movement.

Characteristics:

- embedded in megaframe,
- horizontal + diagonal + vertical,
- autonomous shared capsules,
- branching guideways,
- transfer rings / civic nodes,
- no conventional rail station language.

It should not resemble:

- train,
- monorail,
- elevator,
- roller coaster.

## 12.2 Aerial mobility

Strong secondary visual language.

Use:

- controlled number of craft,
- organized aerial corridors,
- architectural docking,
- vertical transfer,
- suspended arrival structures.

Avoid:

- flying-car traffic jam,
- cars with wings,
- aircraft pasted into empty sky.

Architecture must visibly respond to aerial mobility.

## 12.3 Water mobility

Must feel part of the same network.

Use:

- autonomous hydrofoil / low-wake craft,
- adaptive docking,
- multimodal interchange,
- direct connection to civic / magnetic systems.

Avoid conventional ferry-terminal design.

## 12.4 Ground

The main civic areas are largely post-road.

Do not allow asphalt road networks to dominate hero views.

Service movement may exist, but it should not organize the visible public city.

---

# 13. Waterfront taste

Tokyo Bay is a main protagonist.

The waterfront is not:

> building → promenade → seawall → water.

It is a thick **amphibious civic edge**.

Use:

- tidal terraces,
- floodable public landscapes,
- adaptive platforms,
- ecological islands,
- habitat infrastructure,
- water filtration,
- adjustable mobility docks,
- civic structures extending over water,
- climate-response landscapes.

Open water remains important.

Do not fill the bay with objects.

The future should feel like Tokyo has learned how to **live with water**, not simply defend itself from water.

---

# 14. Ecology taste

Nature is infrastructure.

Avoid:

- random green walls,
- decorative planter trees,
- generic rooftop gardens,
- excessive “eco-futurist” vines.

Prefer:

- climate canopy,
- habitat corridor,
- coastal wetland,
- stormwater system,
- deep structural garden,
- shade ecosystem,
- elevated habitat,
- water-retention landscape,
- botanical climate chamber.

Real trees remain welcome.

But vegetation must feel integrated into a larger environmental system.

---

# 15. Media taste

## MEDIA IS ARCHITECTURE, NOT ADVERTISING

Fuji TV's media heritage remains important.

Use:

- responsive architectural surfaces,
- civic information embedded into material,
- variable-opacity envelopes,
- cultural broadcasting surfaces,
- environmental data,
- public media chambers.

Avoid:

- Times Square,
- Shibuya billboard density,
- giant commercial advertisements,
- random floating holograms,
- glowing UI everywhere.

Technology should feel mature enough that it no longer needs to constantly advertise itself.

---

# 16. Material taste

Core material family:

- pale advanced structural composites,
- brushed silver metal,
- off-white ceramic / mineral surfaces,
- selective high-quality glass,
- smart translucent membrane,
- variable-opacity environmental envelope,
- dark precision structural details,
- natural vegetation,
- blue-grey Tokyo Bay water.

Avoid making every occupied volume a glass box.

Glass should reveal important interior life, not act as the default building material.

Material quality comes from:

- precision,
- depth,
- joints,
- layering,
- light response,
- believable construction,
- restraint.

---

# 17. Color taste

Primary palette:

- pale silver,
- warm off-white,
- light mineral grey,
- restrained dark structure,
- clear / blue-grey glass,
- natural ecological green,
- blue-grey bay.

Accent light is functional.

Avoid dominant:

- purple,
- magenta,
- saturated cyan,
- neon rainbow palettes.

The city should remain futuristic in daylight with most lights turned off.

---

# 18. Lighting taste

Master reference condition:

## CLEAR LATE AFTERNOON — TOKYO

Use:

- warm directional sunlight,
- bright open sky,
- readable long shadows,
- maritime atmospheric softness,
- clear material response,
- strong depth.

Do not depend on:

- fog,
- darkness,
- bloom,
- neon,
- dramatic cinematic grading

to make the city feel futuristic.

Daylight is the primary truth.

Night should reveal systems that already exist in daylight, not invent a different city.

---

# 19. Human scale

Odaiba 2127 is inhabited, but not crowded.

Use people to show:

- enormous structural scale,
- civic use,
- social comfort,
- mobility transfer,
- public activity.

Prefer:

- small groups,
- multiple types of activity,
- significant negative space.

Avoid:

- empty architecture render,
- festival-like crowd density,
- Shibuya-style pedestrian saturation.

The city should feel calm, civic and active.

---

# 20. Secondary district rule

Not every structure should be a landmark.

Secondary buildings must prove that the world can continue beyond the Fuji civic core.

They should:

- have different silhouettes,
- serve different functions,
- use different combinations of the shared DNA,
- remain related through systems rather than copy-pasted forms.

Avoid:

- one sphere per building,
- identical white megaframes,
- repetitive glass towers,
- generic futuristic skyline filler.

If the main landmark is removed from the image, the district should still look like Odaiba 2127.

---

# 21. Radical / familiar balance

Target:

- approximately **60% understandable**
- approximately **40% unfamiliar**

The viewer should recognize:

- public space,
- movement,
- structure,
- ecology,
- civic use.

But some hybrid systems should have no simple 2027 name.

This creates:

## PLAUSIBLE UNFAMILIARITY

Too familiar = 2027.

Too unfamiliar = fantasy.

---

# 22. Forbidden shortcuts

Do not use these as substitutes for actual future design:

- adding flying vehicles to a contemporary city,
- adding glass,
- adding neon,
- making structures bigger,
- adding trees,
- adding holograms,
- adding curved shapes,
- adding skybridges,
- adding LED screens,
- making everything white,
- making every object spherical.

A future object must change the surrounding urban logic.

---

# 23. Design workflow for agents

For every new building, evolved building, or major object:

## Step 1 — Identify its role
What does it do in Odaiba 2127?

## Step 2 — Identify the 2027 archetype
What would this object/building normally be in 2027?

## Step 3 — Replace the archetype
How has that typology fundamentally changed after 100 years?

## Step 4 — Connect it to city systems
How does it connect to:

- structure,
- mobility,
- ecology,
- waterfront,
- public life,
- media?

## Step 5 — Apply Fuji-derived DNA
Choose the relevant lineage:

- frame,
- suspension,
- circular geometry,
- diagonal circulation,
- void,
- elevated connection.

Do not use all of them automatically.

## Step 6 — Check the references
Compare against R01–R06.

## Step 7 — Run the 2127 test
Could this still plausibly be a 2027 project with cosmetic changes?

If yes, redesign it.

# 24. Acceptance checklist

A new design is ready only if the answer is YES to the relevant items below.

### World
- [ ] Clearly belongs to the same world as R01.
- [ ] Does not depend on the main Fuji landmark to establish identity.
- [ ] Feels more layered, not simply more crowded.

### Future
- [ ] Changes the underlying typology, not only styling.
- [ ] Contains spatial or system logic that would be unusual or impossible in 2027.
- [ ] Still has understandable engineering logic.

### Architecture
- [ ] Shows Fuji-derived DNA without literal copying.
- [ ] Avoids generic contemporary glass architecture.
- [ ] Avoids repetitive slab + cylindrical column language.
- [ ] Preserves major voids and openness.

### Mobility
- [ ] Mobility visibly affects architectural form where relevant.
- [ ] Does not rely on conventional road / station logic.
- [ ] Aerial craft, if present, are integrated rather than decorative.

### Ecology
- [ ] Nature performs a system-level function.
- [ ] Greenery is not merely decorative.

### Waterfront
- [ ] If near Tokyo Bay, land and water interact as one civic system.
- [ ] The waterfront is not a conventional promenade with futuristic props.

### Media
- [ ] Media is architectural and civic.
- [ ] No advertising clutter or cyberpunk UI.

### Human experience
- [ ] Human scale remains understandable.
- [ ] Public space feels usable.
- [ ] The scene is inhabited but not overcrowded.

### Taste
- [ ] Radical systems.
- [ ] Restrained aesthetics.
- [ ] Plausible unfamiliarity.
- [ ] Still convincing in clear daylight.

# 25. Final design statement

Every addition to Odaiba 2127 should feel as if it emerged from one hundred years of continuous urban evolution.

The city should not look like the future imagined by decorating today's architecture.

It should look like a city whose:

- architecture,
- mobility,
- ecology,
- public space,
- waterfront,
- and media systems

have all evolved together.

The final visual reaction should always be:

> **“I understand where this city came from, but its operating logic clearly belongs to another century.”**

And specifically:

> **“This could only be Odaiba 2127.”**
